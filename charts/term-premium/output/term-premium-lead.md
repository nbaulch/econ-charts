The 10-year Treasury yield rose 0.9 percentage point over the past year, to 5.01 percent, and the Fed's two main models disagree on why. The New York Fed model attributes nearly all of the rise to higher expected policy rates; the Fed Board model attributes 0.5 point to a higher term premium. The main reason is that the Board model follows professional forecasters, who lowered their expected 10-year average bill rate to 3.0 percent this year from 3.2 percent while markets moved the other way. Both models agree the term premium has risen about 0.9 point over two years.
