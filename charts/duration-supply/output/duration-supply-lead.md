Big tech companies have become a significant new source of long-term debt for investors to absorb, while Treasury's contribution has not grown. Measured in 10-year equivalents—the amount of 10-year notes that would carry the same risk from changes in interest rates—bonds sold by Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Oracle added $255 billion to private holdings in the 12 months to August 2026, 26 percent as much as Treasury's $980 billion. Before then, big tech's bonds had never added more than $61 billion in 12 months. Treasury's addition compares with $1,004 billion a year earlier and a peak of $1,221 billion in December 2024. The figures leave out private placements, such as the financing for Meta's Hyperion data center, so they understate big tech's borrowing.
