**Data centers and power:** Construction of data centers and of electric power and other power facilities.

**Software:** Business investment in software, both purchased and developed in house.

**Computers:** Business investment in computers and peripheral equipment.

**Net trade in computers:** Exports minus imports of computers and parts, counting only the capital goods share of trade.

Over the four quarters through 2026 Q2, AI-related investment added 0.66 point a year to growth before computer trade and 0.45 after, so trade offset 31 percent. Counting computer trade by businesses' share of U.S. spending on computers puts the offset at 37 percent, and valuing it at the prices of business investment in computers puts it at 48 percent.

In 2026 Q2, computer trade added to growth even though spending on imported computers rose, because import prices rose about twice as fast as prices of business investment in computers.

Source: Bureau of Economic Analysis, National Income and Product Accounts, data through 2026 Q2. Method from Paul E. Soto, Mason Thieu, and Jeffrey S. Allen, ["The AI Buildout and the Economy"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-ai-buildout-and-the-economy-publicly-available-data-to-assess-ais-impact-20260717.html), FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, July 2026.
